# Patterns that make English prose read as machine-written

Each entry: what it looks like, why it hurts, and the fix. Quoted examples are for spotting the
pattern; never leave them in output.

## Stock words

Replace with the plain word, or cut:

| Stock word | Plain choice |
|---|---|
| delve into, dive into | look at, cover |
| leverage, utilize | use |
| facilitate | help, let |
| empower, enable | let |
| streamline | simplify, speed up |
| robust, seamless, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art | say what it does |
| pivotal, paramount, crucial, vital | important, or show why |
| transformative, game-changing, revolutionary | say what changed |
| landscape, realm, tapestry, ecosystem (figurative) | field, area, or cut |
| embark on, unlock, elevate, supercharge | start, get, improve |
| meticulous, intricate, multifaceted | careful, detailed, or show it |
| navigate (figurative), foster, harness | handle, build, use |

Intensifiers that usually add nothing: *really, very, truly, actually, simply, just, literally,
incredibly, extremely, fundamentally, essentially*. Keep one when it carries real contrast or the
writer's speech rhythm.

## Empty phrases

Cut and start with the content: *it's worth noting that, it's important to remember, when it
comes to, in today's fast-paced world, at the end of the day, in terms of, the fact that, in order
to (→ to), as a matter of fact, needless to say, let's dive in, without further ado, I hope this
message finds you well, please don't hesitate to reach out, feel free to*.

## Padding

- **Announcing**: "In this post I will explain…", "Below you'll find…". Just explain.
- **Restating**: the same point again in other words, often after "In other words" or "Put
  simply". Keep the clearer version.
- **Hedge stacking**: "might potentially perhaps help". One hedge, if any.
- **Over-politeness**: thanking, apologising and offering further help in one message. One line
  at most, and only where the relationship needs it.
- **Generic context**: "Communication is key in any team." Fails the portability test.

## Sentence-level tics

**False contrast.** "It's not about speed — it's about trust." / "This isn't a tool, it's a
mindset." State the real claim: "Users stay because they trust the numbers."

**Teaser setup.** "Here's the thing:", "What nobody tells you is…", "The secret?". Delete the
setup; the claim stands alone.

**Colon reveal.** "The result: a 3x faster build." Write a sentence: "The build is now three times
faster." Keep colons for lists, labels and quotes.

**Inflated significance.** "marks a turning point", "is a testament to", "plays a key role in",
"underscores the importance of". Give the fact and let the reader judge.

**Hollow -ing tail.** "…, highlighting our commitment to quality." / "…, reflecting the team's
values." Replace with the concrete consequence or cut.

**Vague attribution.** "Studies show", "experts agree", "many believe". Name the source. If there
is none, cut the claim or ask the user for one.

**Reader steering.** "This is key.", "Notice that…", "Importantly,", "As you can see,". Show why
it matters instead of announcing that it does.

**Rhetorical question answered at once.** "Why does this matter? Because…". State the answer.

**Staccato drama.** "We built it. We shipped it. It worked." Join into a normal sentence unless
the rhythm is the writer's own.

**Triplets everywhere.** Lists of exactly three adjectives or clauses as a reflex ("fast, simple,
and reliable"). Keep only the items that are true and specific.

**Fake-active subjects.** "The data tells a story", "The roadmap reflects our ambition". Give the
action to a person: "We planned three releases."

## Endings

- **Recap**: "In summary…", "Overall…", a final paragraph repeating the body. Cut it.
- **Mic-drop line**: an aphorism or metaphor added for weight ("And that makes all the
  difference."). Delete it; end on the last concrete sentence or the next step.
- **Open-ended offer**: "Let me know if you have any questions!" Keep only when the channel
  expects it, once.

## Formatting

- Headings, bullets and bold only when the text is long enough to need navigation. A short
  message is prose.
- No emoji in headings; no bold scattered through sentences for emphasis.
- Em dashes: none in short messages; one or two in long text, only where a comma or full stop
  reads worse. Break up any cluster.
- After a colon, continue in lower case unless a proper noun, a full quoted sentence or code
  follows.
